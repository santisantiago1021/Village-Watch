Recurring Issues Worth Knowing About

Patterns across 2025–2026

💰Property at 1 Helen Keller Way — no price ever disclosed

In January 2025 the Village authorized a $10 million bond for "acquisition of real property" — no property named. Six weeks later, they voted to buy 1 Helen Keller Way — no purchase price stated. Engineering and renovation contracts followed throughout 2025-2026, also without disclosed amounts. The public record still contains no figure for what this building cost.

2025: Jan 7 (bond), Feb 18 (purchase). 2026: multiple engineering contracts.

🔒Litigation settled in secret — every month

After nearly every closed-door executive session, the Board votes to approve "pending litigation" settlements. In 2025, at least 8 separate matters were approved this way. In 2026, at least 7 more. None include the other party's name, the case description, or the dollar amount. The Village Attorney's authority to settle cases without Board approval at all was also raised from $5,000 to $15,000 in October 2025.

Confirmed in: Mar 4, Apr 15, May 6, Jun 17, Jul 1, Oct 7 (2025); Feb 17, May 19, Aug 3, Aug 18 (2026).

📉Tax settlements reduce the Village's revenue — repeatedly

Property owners who believe their assessments are too high can challenge them in court. The Village settles many of these cases with a cash refund and a locked-in lower assessment for up to 3 future years — permanently reducing what those properties pay. Confirmed refunds: at least $145,900 in 2025 and $146,300+ in 2026, with ongoing assessment cuts on top.

Tax refunds appear at nearly every BOT meeting both years.

🏗️Consultants hired repeatedly, amounts often missing

Several firms receive repeated contracts without competitive rebids and without disclosed amounts: Laberge Group (at least $165K+ in addenda in 2025 alone, plus undisclosed amounts), H2M Architects (8+ scopes in 2025, most without amounts), Prioleau Development Group ($10,000/month confirmed in 2026, original 2025 contract had no amount), Greenberg Traurig LLP (lobbying, no amount), Davidoff Hutcher & Citron (lobbying, no amount).

Ongoing across 2025–2026.

🏠Two large apartment buildings approved downtown

The Planning Board approved two significant mixed-use developments in 2025: 16 Cooper St (5 stories, approved July 21) and 257 Main St (7 stories, approved September 15 with 10% attainable housing requirement, final SEQRA clearance December 15). Both are the largest private development approvals of the year.

2025: Planning Board Jul 21, Sep 15, Dec 15.

🗳️Good Cause Eviction failed — but it was close

A local law that would have given Hempstead renters stronger protections against evictions failed on November 3, 2025 by a 2-3 vote. Trustees Burroughs and Whitaker voted yes; Deputy Mayor Daniels, Trustee Carter, and Mayor Hobbs voted no. This is the clearest ideological split in the 2025-2026 record.

2025: Nov 3 BOT meeting.

📷Flock Safety surveillance — no price ever disclosed

The Village signed a contract with Flock Safety in January 2025 for license plate reader cameras — no amount stated. In June 2025 they added a gunshot detection system — no amount stated. In February 2026 they extended the contract, claiming savings of "over $1 million" but still disclosing no contract value or total spend.

2025: Jan 21, Jun 17. 2026: Feb 3.

⚖️Trustee Burroughs — conflict of interest question

In June 2025, Trustee Noah Burroughs filed a Zoning Board application for his own property at 274 Rhodes Ave (Case #2164) to convert it to a mother/daughter unit. The ZBA approved it in July 2025. The public record does not indicate whether the Board of Trustees took any conflict-of-interest step. Burroughs also sat out several key votes where he appeared to have a personal interest.

2025: ZBA Jun 5 (filed), Jul 3 (approved). BOT Oct 21, Nov 18 (Burroughs abstained on own pension records).

🏊$30M Mirschel Park + $17.4M Kennedy Pool — limited cost disclosure

Two major capital projects are underway. The Mirschel Park Community Center (total project cost: $30M; $15M bond authorized June 2025; $675K + $900K in grants applied for) and the Kennedy Memorial Park Pool renovation ($17.4M in contracts awarded August 2026). Engineering, design, and management costs for both projects are largely undisclosed across dozens of contract resolutions.

2025: Jun 17, Jul 15. 2026: Jan–Aug (multiple resolutions).

FLAG Undisclosed settlements: 14+ executive session settlements approved 2025–26 with no public dollar amount. A separate $1M Judgments & Claims reserve is budgeted annually — but actual settlement costs are never disclosed line by line.
BOND $25M+ in bond authorizations (2025–26) will grow future debt service. The FY27 transfer to the Debt Fund is $5.3M — up from $4.9M in FY25 actuals. No 5-year projection was disclosed in meeting minutes.
PAYMENT 1 Helen Keller Way purchase price still undisclosed. A $10M bond was authorized January 2025 for "acquisition of real property." The building was purchased in February 2025. No purchase price appears in any public record reviewed.
TAX $517K+ in confirmed tax certiorari refunds (2025–26) reduce the tax base. Multi-year assessment reductions from settled cases add ongoing revenue loss not reflected in one-time refund figures.
CAPITAL $47.4M in capital projects underway (Mirschel Park $30M + Kennedy Pool $17.4M). Most individual engineering, design, and management contracts were approved without disclosed amounts in the meeting minutes.
TAX CAP State property tax cap override approved February 2026. New York's GML §3-c limits how much a municipality can raise its tax levy each year (generally 2% or inflation, whichever is lower). A 60% supermajority BOT vote can override it. The Village voted to exceed the cap at the February 17, 2026 meeting — meaning the FY2027 tax levy of $71.0M increased beyond what the state limit would allow without that override. The FY2027 levy is up from approximately $66M in FY2025 actuals. The budget has grown faster than the cap permits in recent years, with the override used as the workaround. Source: Feb 17, 2026 BOT meeting ↗

Open Questions

Things the village has never addressed — worth asking

These are not accusations. They are questions any taxpayer should be able to get a straight answer to — and hasn't. Each one is grounded in a specific line item or documented fact from the public record.

🔴 Ammunition budget: nearly doubled, with almost nothing spent — why?

The FY2026 ammunition budget (line 3120-430) was $13,129. Actual spending through February 15, 2026 — more than 8 months into the fiscal year — was $102. The FY2027 budget then jumps to $28,150, more than doubling the prior year allocation.

For a 132-officer department, mandatory annual qualification alone typically requires tens of thousands of rounds. If they spent $102 in 8.5 months, one of the following must be true: (a) qualification rounds were funded through another line or a federal supply program; (b) officers did not qualify on schedule; or (c) the prior budgets were inflated. None of these has been explained publicly. The budget increase to $28,150 — following a year where essentially nothing was spent — raises an additional question about what the money is actually budgeted for.

Source: FY 2026–27 Adopted Budget, line 3120-430 (Ammunition) ↗

🔴 Police overtime: $4.3M spent vs. $3.0M budgeted — every year

In FY2026, the Village budgeted $3,000,000 for police overtime and spent $4,289,632 — 43% over budget. The FY2027 overtime budget is again set at $3,000,000. This pattern suggests the village is either intentionally underbudgeting overtime, or there is a structural issue driving perpetual overruns that has not been addressed.

Overtime overruns happened in the same period that Flock Safety cameras, gunshot detection, and a drone-as-first-responder program were added — all justified, at least in part, by efficiency and crime-reduction benefits. If those tools are working as claimed, why has overtime consistently exceeded budget?

Source: FY 2026–27 Adopted Budget, line 3120-115 (Overtime) ↗

🟡 Flock Safety: "over $1M in savings" — savings compared to what?

The February 3, 2026 BOT resolution extending the Flock Safety contract states the extension will result in savings of "over $1 million." The original contract value was never publicly disclosed. The new contract value was never publicly disclosed. Without knowing the baseline cost, the savings claim is mathematically unverifiable.

The Village has had three separate votes touching the Flock Safety contract and has never stated in a public record how much the contract costs per year or in total.

Source: BOT Minutes, February 3, 2026 ↗

🟡 The "Systems Maintenance" line: $0 budgeted → $856,195 in one year

Police dept line 3120-402 (Systems Maintenance) had no FY2026 budget allocation. Actual spend through February 2026 was $683. The FY2027 budget allocates $856,195 for this same line. That is an increase of over $855,000 with no public explanation in any meeting record.

No BOT resolution, no agenda item, and no public discussion explaining what this line covers or why it is needed at this scale appeared in the 77 meeting records reviewed. Given the simultaneous expansion of Flock Safety's contract, this line is a natural place to ask questions.

Source: FY 2026–27 Adopted Budget, line 3120-402 ↗
