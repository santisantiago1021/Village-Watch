Recurring Issues Worth Knowing About

Patterns across 2025–2026

💰Property at 1 Helen Keller Way — no price ever disclosed

In January 2025 the Village authorized a $10 million bond for "acquisition of real property" — no property named. Six weeks later, they voted to buy 1 Helen Keller Way — no purchase price stated. Engineering and renovation contracts followed throughout 2025-2026, also without disclosed amounts. The public record still contains no figure for what this building cost.

2025: Jan 7 (bond), Feb 18 (purchase). 2026: multiple engineering contracts.

🔒Litigation settled in secret — every month

After nearly every closed-door executive session, the Board votes to approve "pending litigation" settlements. In 2025, at least 8 separate matters were approved this way. In 2026, at least 7 more. None include the other party's name, the case description, or the dollar amount. The Village Attorney's authority to settle cases without Board approval at all was also raised from $5,000 to $15,000 in October 2025.

Confirmed in: Mar 4, Apr 15, May 6, Jun 17, Jul 1, Oct 7 (2025); Feb 17, May 19, Aug 3, Aug 18 (2026).

📉Tax settlements reduce the Village's revenue — repeatedly

Property owners who believe their assessments are too high can challenge them in court. The Village settles many of these cases with a cash refund and a locked-in lower assessment for up to 3 future years — permanently reducing what those properties pay. Confirmed refunds: at least $145,900 in 2025 and $146,300+ in 2026, with ongoing assessment cuts on top.

Tax refunds appear at nearly every BOT meeting both years.

🏗️Consultants hired repeatedly, amounts often missing

Several firms receive repeated contracts without competitive rebids and without disclosed amounts: Laberge Group (at least $165K+ in addenda in 2025 alone, plus undisclosed amounts), H2M Architects (8+ scopes in 2025, most without amounts), Prioleau Development Group ($10,000/month confirmed in 2026, original 2025 contract had no amount), Greenberg Traurig LLP (lobbying, no amount), Davidoff Hutcher & Citron (lobbying, no amount).

Ongoing across 2025–2026.

🏠Two large apartment buildings approved downtown

The Planning Board approved two significant mixed-use developments in 2025: 16 Cooper St (5 stories, approved July 21) and 257 Main St (7 stories, approved September 15 with 10% attainable housing requirement, final SEQRA clearance December 15). Both are the largest private development approvals of the year.

2025: Planning Board Jul 21, Sep 15, Dec 15.

🗳️Good Cause Eviction failed — but it was close

A local law that would have given Hempstead renters stronger protections against evictions failed on November 3, 2025 by a 2-3 vote. Trustees Burroughs and Whitaker voted yes; Deputy Mayor Daniels, Trustee Carter, and Mayor Hobbs voted no. This is the clearest ideological split in the 2025-2026 record.

2025: Nov 3 BOT meeting.

📷Flock Safety surveillance — no price ever disclosed

The Village signed a contract with Flock Safety in January 2025 for license plate reader cameras — no amount stated. In June 2025 they added a gunshot detection system — no amount stated. In February 2026 they extended the contract, claiming savings of "over $1 million" but still disclosing no contract value or total spend.

2025: Jan 21, Jun 17. 2026: Feb 3.

⚖️Trustee Burroughs — conflict of interest question

In June 2025, Trustee Noah Burroughs filed a Zoning Board application for his own property at 274 Rhodes Ave (Case #2164) to convert it to a mother/daughter unit. The ZBA approved it in July 2025. The public record does not indicate whether the Board of Trustees took any conflict-of-interest step. Burroughs also sat out several key votes where he appeared to have a personal interest.

2025: ZBA Jun 5 (filed), Jul 3 (approved). BOT Oct 21, Nov 18 (Burroughs abstained on own pension records).

🏊$30M Mirschel Park + $17.4M Kennedy Pool — limited cost disclosure

Two major capital projects are underway. The Mirschel Park Community Center (total project cost: $30M; $15M bond authorized June 2025; $675K + $900K in grants applied for) and the Kennedy Memorial Park Pool renovation ($17.4M in contracts awarded August 2026). Engineering, design, and management costs for both projects are largely undisclosed across dozens of contract resolutions.

2025: Jun 17, Jul 15. 2026: Jan–Aug (multiple resolutions).
