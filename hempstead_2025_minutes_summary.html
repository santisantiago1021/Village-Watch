Village of Hempstead — 2025 Meeting Minutes Summary

Board of Trustees · Planning Board · Zoning Board  ·  Compiled from villageofhempstead.org/AgendaCenter  ·  Last updated Aug 25, 2025

Coverage note: This file covers 48 sets of minutes: all 26 BOT regular/special/annual meetings (Jan 7 – Dec 16), all 11 Planning Board meetings (Jan 27 – Dec 15), and all 11 Zoning Board meetings (Jan 2 – Dec 4). The Dec 2 BOT minutes URL (ID 1113) returned the same content as Dec 16 — both appear to be the Dec 16 meeting. The Dec 2 meeting's distinct content (Hempstead Convenience Store 2 Corp. hearing) is referenced in Dec 16 minutes as having been held Dec 2, confirming it occurred but its separate minutes were not accessible. Note that the Nov 18 BOT minutes page included hearing on Good Cause Eviction (not Oct 21 as originally scheduled).

Patterns Worth Watching

  • ▶ $10M bond for unnamed property + undisclosed purchase price at 1 Helen Keller Way. Jan 7: $10M bond authorized for "acquisition of real property" — no property named. Feb 18: purchase contract executed for 1 Helen Keller Way — unanimous vote, no purchase price in resolution. Jun 3 and Nov 18: H2M authorized for asbestos survey and construction documents for roof replacement, electrical, HVAC, elevator (combined ~$14,065 in engineering fees). The full acquisition cost and renovation budget remain undisclosed in public minutes through year-end.
  • ▶ Two consultants hired on 3-2 votes to study the same City of Hempstead transformation question. Feb 18: Laberge Group retained (3-2: Daniels/Burroughs/Hobbs vs. Griffith/Boone). Apr 1: CGR (Center for Governmental Research) also retained on the same 3-2 split. No report or findings from either consultant appeared in any subsequent 2025 meeting minutes. Neither contract disclosed a dollar amount.
  • ▶ Executive session followed immediately by undisclosed litigation vote — 5 confirmed instances in 2025. Mar 4: 3 undisclosed matters. Apr 15: 1 matter. May 6: 1 matter. Jun 17: 1 matter (Burroughs ABSTAINED — unusual). Jul 1: 1 matter. Oct 7: 1 matter. None carry public amounts or party names. The Village Attorney's settlement authority was also increased from $5,000 to $15,000 (Oct 7) — matters below that threshold will never require Board approval at all.
  • ▶ Trustee Burroughs attendance pattern — frequent absences or late arrivals, full year 2025. Jan 21 (Zoom), Mar 4 (ABSENT), Apr 1 (ABSENT), Apr 7 (ABSENT), Apr 15 (ABSENT), May 6 (ABSENT), May 20 (ABSENT), Jun 17 (arrived late 6:29 pm for 5:45 pm hearing), Jul 1 (present), Sep 16 (present but Daniels+Carter EXCUSED), Oct 21 (present but entered executive session to discuss his own pension record — ROA form approved with Burroughs abstaining), Dec 16 (arrived late 6:37 pm for 6:15 pm hearing). Jun 5: Burroughs ZBA application case #2164 — his own variance for 274 Rhodes Ave was approved with conditions July 3.
  • ▶ Tax certiorari settlements at nearly every BOT meeting, with prospective assessment locks. 2025 confirmed refunds: $3,000 (Madonna Holdings, Jan 7), $3,800 (535 S. Franklin, Feb 18), $17,000 (415 S. Franklin, May 6), $22,500 (Mark Wieboldt 416 Clinton, May 6), $22,000 (Fulton 215 LLC 131-133F Fulton, May 20), $2,800 (BP Products / Look Oil 375 Clinton, Jun 3), $9,750 (Hempstead LGL 16 Burr St, Aug 4), $5,000 (Hempstead LGL 92 Union Pl, Aug 4), $12,500 (Christian Castillo 140 Front St, Sep 2), $28,000 (316-318 Fulton LG LLC, Nov 3), plus J.M. Main Street $19,550 (Mar 19). Running 2025 total: at least $145,900 in direct refunds plus cascading assessment reductions locked for 3 years each.
  • ▶ Business license revolving door — suspensions opened and reversed in the same cycle. Multiple licenses were suspended then restored via stipulation (Reyna's Kitchen, Rafa Deli, JER Deli, Luis Lopez/Chase Collision — all reversed Apr 1 after hearings through Jan-Mar). Sep 2: Brothers 3 Deli suspension resolved via stipulation. Sep 16: AAAA Nassau Inc. tow license suspension reversed (after 3 adjournments spanning Jun 17 – Sep 2). Dec 16: Hempstead Convenience Store 2 Corp. license revoked — this was upheld, making it an outlier where revocation actually stuck.
  • ▶ Laberge Group receiving repeated addenda with no competitive rebid — full 2025 picture. Jan 7 (+$7,500 Mirschel Park), Jan 21 (rate update + DOZ benchmark — no amount), Mar 19 (+$4,500 × 7 priority projects = $31,500), Apr 1 (+$126,500 Mirschel Park expanded design), Jun 3 (grant management services 2025-2026 — no amount). Cumulative disclosed addenda: at least $165,500, with additional undisclosed amounts. No competitive rebid process mentioned.
  • ▶ Flock Safety contract — original signed Jan 21, 2025, no amount disclosed; expanded Jun 17, 2025. Jan 21: initial Flock agreement for license plate readers (no amount). Jun 17: new agreement for Gunshot Detection system added (no amount). Both approved unanimously; neither disclosed a contract value. The Feb 2026 extension (shown in the 2026 file) claims savings of "over $1M" but still discloses no contract cost.
  • ▶ Major capital projects approved with no total budget disclosed. Jun 17: $15M bond authorized for Mirschel Park Community Center; total project cost stated as $30M. Jul 15 (Special): EPF grant application for Mirschel Park ($675,000 requested). Kennedy Park Pool: SEQRA Negative Declaration and DASNY grant approved (Apr 15); H2M engineering for pool authorized (Apr 15, no amount); ASHRAE Level II energy audit authorized (Aug 19 Special, no amount). Clinton Street Treatment Plant peer review also authorized Aug 19 with no amount.
  • ▶ Prioleau Development Group hired Jun 17 as owner representative for Village construction projects — no amount disclosed. This is the same firm that receives $10,000/month per the 2026 file (Jul 7, 2026). The 2025 agreement (Jun 17) did not disclose any rate or contract value. No competitive bid process mentioned.
  • ▶ Residency waivers routine — confirmed 2025 instances. Jun 17: Adam Alvarez (Seasonal), Keren Shriki (Cashier I). Jul 15 Special: Demetra Ingram (Village Auditor). Aug 4: Ayana Raiford + Daniel Clark (Seasonal), Allison Saavedra (Typist-Clerk Bilingual). Sep 2: Kimberly McLean (Deputy Village Attorney). Sep 16: Gregory Antoszyk (Plumbing Inspector). Nov 3: Robert Engel (Plumbing Inspector), Julianne Pulizzi (Cashier I). At least 10 individual residency waivers in 2025.
  • ▶ Good Cause Eviction Law (Local Law #5 of 2025) — failed 2-3 in November. The law was proposed at the Oct 21 BOT meeting (public hearing held) and voted on Nov 3. Motion FAILED: Burroughs + Whitaker voted YES; Daniels + Carter + Mayor Hobbs voted NO. This is the clearest ideological split in 2025 voting — the Mayor and Deputy Mayor sided with Carter to defeat the tenant protection law.
  • ▶ Trustee Burroughs ZBA variance — potential conflict of interest not noted. Jun 5: Case #2164 filed by Noah Burroughs for 274 Rhodes Ave — convert single-family to mother/daughter. Jul 3: case approved with conditions (Burroughs absent from ZBA meeting — he does not sit on ZBA, but the public record does not indicate whether the BOT took any conflict-of-interest step). This is the same Burroughs who is a sitting Trustee.
  • ▶ Two major mixed-use development approvals at Planning Board. Jul 21: 16 Cooper St — 5-story multi-use apartment building approved (Planning Board also designated Cooper Street as a Type C Street). Sep 15: 257 Main St — 7-story mixed-use apartment building approved, with conditions including 10% attainable housing. These represent the largest private development applications of 2025.
  • ▶ Davidoff Hutcher & Citron LLP hired as lobbyists Sep 16 — no amount disclosed, passed 3-0 with Daniels & Carter excused. The Sep 16 meeting had only Burroughs, Whitaker, and Mayor Hobbs present. All substantive items (including this lobbying contract, the student resource officer contract amendment, and the IMEG increase) were approved by a quorum of 3 with no public amount.
